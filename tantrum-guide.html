Calm in the Storm

A science-backed tantrum response plan for Indian parents

✔ Why tantrums are neurological, not behavioural

✔ What to say in the hardest moments

✔ A 3-step system that actually works

Your child is not misbehaving.

They are overwhelmed.

Most advice tells you to distract, scold, or ignore.
Short-term, it may stop the noise.
Long-term, it doesn’t build regulation.

What you need is not more advice.
You need to understand what is happening inside your child’s brain.

Try this today

Next tantrum:

• Don’t speak for 10 seconds
• Sit near them
• Slow your breathing

Most parents react immediately.
Regulation begins when you pause.

What’s happening in their brain

Emotional brain: ON
Thinking brain: OFF

You cannot reason with a child in a tantrum.
First, you help them calm.

Why your approach isn’t working

“Stop crying”
Result: Emotion increases

Distraction
Result: No regulation learned

Threats
Result: Fear + more chaos

Over-explaining
Result: Brain not listening

The 3-step system

1. Regulate yourself
Lower voice. Slow breathing.

2. Name emotion
“You’re upset right now”

Do not add “but”

3. Hold limit
“I won’t let you hit”

The goal is not to stop emotions.
It is to teach what to do with them.

What to say

The right sentence matters more than 20 wrong ones.

Public meltdown
“You really wanted that. I’m here.”

Hitting
“I won’t let you hit. You’re angry.”

Crying
“You’re safe. I’m here.”

Prevent tantrums

Patterns are predictable:
hunger · fatigue · overstimulation

Use routines

Give limited choices
